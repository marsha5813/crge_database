Japan: Context (1940-1950)

Governmental Regulations on Religion and Legal Cases

1. Overall, how strict were the regulations on religion or aspects of religion during this period, and to what extent did the state suppress religion? Was there, in effect, a state religion? Were there any privileged religions during this decade? (500–1000 characters)

The 1940s was an era marked by wartime and postwar transitions. In the prewar period, the Peace Preservation Law was amended in 1941. This revision broadened the scope of punishable offenses, targeting not only critical speech and actions against the government, but also colonial independence movements and new religious movements that rejected the emperor system—in short, anything contrary to state policy was subject to suppression.

After World War II, various reforms were implemented by the Occupation forces, fundamentally restructuring Japan’s prewar religious landscape (Nishimura, 17). In 1946, the new Japanese Constitution was promulgated, granting freedom of religion and related rights. The directive concerning religious freedom from the General Headquarters (GHQ) of the Allied Forces was delivered to the Japanese government in early October 1945. GHQ ordered the repeal of laws such as the Peace Preservation Law and the Religious Organizations Law. It also granted freedom of religious education in private schools and revised the relationship between the state and religion. On December 28, the Religious Corporations Ordinance was enacted to replace the old Religious Organizations Law, aiming to safeguard the autonomy and property of religious groups.

  1. 1940年代は、戦中戦後という時代であり、戦前には1941年に治安維持法の改正があった。この改正により、これまでよりも処罰の対象が拡大し、政府に対する批判的な言動や活動、植民地独立運動、天皇制を否定する新興宗教など国家の方針に反する全てが取締りの対象となった。

　第二次世界大戦後は、占領軍による様々な改革がなされ、戦前の宗教構造が根本的に改編された（Nishimura, 17) 。そして、 1946年には日本国憲法が交付され、信教の自由などが認められた。宗教の自由に関する連合軍総司令部の指令が日本政府に対して伝えられたのは、1945年10月初旬であり、GHQは、日本政府に対し、治安維持法をはじめとした宗教団体法などの解除を発令し、さらに私立学校における宗教教育の自由を認め、国家と宗教との関係を改め、12月28日には宗教団体法に代わる宗教法人令を公布し、宗教団体の秩序と財産の保全を確保しようとした。

　Specifically, on October 4, 1945, the “Human Rights Directive” was issued, resulting in the abolition of the Religious Organizations Law in order to remove restrictions on religious freedom and eliminate measures that maintained such restrictions (Nishimura, 17). However, it was considered that the dissolution of religious organizations due to the abolition of the law would in fact hinder religious freedom, so it was necessary to provide an alternative framework (Nishimura, 17). Consequently, on December 28, 1945, the Religious Corporations Ordinance (Imperial Ordinance No. 719 of 1945) was approved by the Cabinet (HARDACRE, 48). Under this new ordinance, religious organizations were granted the right to own property through corporate registration, replacing the former Religious Organizations Law (HARDACRE, 49). From the perspective of religious freedom, the groundbreaking significance of the ordinance lay in securing the social status of faith communities that had previously not been officially recognized as religions. At the same time, however, some groups sought to exploit the simplified registration process for profit-making activities unrelated to religious purposes, taking advantage of tax exemptions on income tax, corporate tax, and land tax (Nishimura, 22–23).

Since 1945, occupation policy concerning religion had been handled by the Civil Information and Education Section (CIE) of the occupation forces, with its Religious Affairs Division in charge of implementation. The Religious Affairs Division’s main concern was Shinto, while MacArthur held a favorable view of Christianity and encouraged missionary activity. When he met with Dr. McKimberly, a Methodist missionary, he emphasized the importance of sending young missionaries to Japan, saying, “If 1,000 missionaries come to Japan, Japan can be Christianized” (Okazaki, 55). Behind this was the larger ideal of realizing the Christian civilizational values that underpinned U.S. foreign policy, and the contemporary international context of promoting anti-communism by democratizing Japan and embedding liberal policies through the occupation (Nishimura, 25). However, staff in the Religious Affairs Division believed it was important to avoid giving the Japanese public the impression that any particular religion was being given preferential treatment (HARDACRE, 49).

具体的には、1945年10月4日、「人権指令」がだされ、宗教的自由を制限したり、その状態を維持させているものを除去するために宗教団体法が廃止さることとなった（Nishimura, 17) しかし、法令の廃止によって宗教団体が解散することになってしまっては、むしろ宗教の自由を阻害することにあたるため、それに代ものを用意することが求められた（Nishimura, 17) そして、1945年12月28日に、宗教法人令（昭和20年勅令第719号）が内閣で承認された（HARDACRE, 48)。これにより、宗教団体方に代わり、宗教団体に法人登記をすることで財産を有する権利を与えることとなった（HARDACRE, 49)　信教の自由の観点からは、これまで宗教と認められなかった信仰共同体の社会的地位を保証したところに宗教法人令の画期的な意義が認められるが、同時のその簡易な手続きに乗じて、宗教目的ではない金儲けのために所得税、法人税、地租の免税措置を利用しようとするものも現れた（Nishimura,22-23)

　1945年以来、宗教に関する占領政策は占領軍の民間情報教育局(CIE)とその下に置かれた宗教課が担当した。宗教課の主な関心は神道にあり、マッカーサーはキリスト教に好意的でキリスト教の宣教師による布教を望んだ。そのため、メソジストのマッキンベリ博士と会見した際には、日本に若い宣教師を送ることの役割と説き「1000人の宣教師が日本に来れば、日本はキリスト教かできる」と語った（Okazaki, 55) その背後には、占領を通して日本を民主化し、自由主義的な政策を定着させることは、米国の対外政策の背景にあるキリスト教文明の理想の実現という大きな理念と反共産主義の推進という同時代の国際情勢からの要請でもあった（Nishimura,25)　そ

しかし、宗教課職員は、特定の宗教が優先されるという印象を日本国民に与えることは避けるべきだ考えた(HARDACRE, 49)

　

In this context, it became necessary to clarify the distinction between “Shinto” as a personal religion and “State Shinto” as constructed by the government. To this end, Captain William Bunce of the Religious Affairs Division utilized the research of Shinto scholar Daniel Holtom and referred to articles on Shinto by Kato Genchi and Kono Shozo published in Monumenta Nipponica by Sophia University in preparing his report (HARDACRE, 50). Bunce’s report stated that State Shinto had been used by Japanese militarists and ultranationalists to cultivate a militaristic spirit among the people and to justify the expansion of war. The report also distinguished between “Shrine Shinto,” “State Shinto,” and “Sect Shinto,” although in the Shinto Directive, the terms “Shrine Shinto” and “State Shinto” were treated as effectively interchangeable (HARDACRE, 51). The report clearly asserted that State Shinto was a religion. It called for severing the relationship between the government and Shinto shrines, treating Shinto on an equal footing with other religions, abolishing the Institute of Divinities (Jingi-in), removing household Shinto altars (kamidana) from schools and public institutions, and halting school visits to shrines (HARDACRE, 51–52).

On December 15, 1945, the “Shinto Directive” was issued, and by the end of January 1946, the Institute of Divinities was abolished, and shrines were released from state control (Nishimura, 18). Furthermore, the Shinto Directive ordered an immediate halt to the provision of public funds for Shinto, the promulgation of militaristic ideology through Shinto doctrines, customs, rites, and ceremonies, the operation of educational institutions for training Shinto clergy and related personnel, and the dissemination of Shinto teachings in school education (HARDACRE, 51).

このようななかで、個人の宗教としての「神道」と国家により創られた「国家神道」の違いを説明する必要がしょうじた。そのため宗教課のウィリアム・バンス大尉は、神道研究者のダニエル・ホルトマンの研究を活用したり、上智大学の『Monument Nipponika』に掲載された加藤玄智や河野省三の神道に関する論文を参考に報告書を作成した（HARDACRE, 50)　バンスの報告書には、国家神道は、日本の軍国主義者や超国家主義者が国民に軍国主義的な精神を養い、戦争の拡大を正当化するために利用された。またこの報告書には、「神社神道」と「国家神道」「教派神道」の差異が記されていたが、神道指令では、実質上「神社神道」は「国家神道」の語は互換可能だとされた（HARDACRE, 51)　そして、報告書には、国家神道は宗教であると断言された。そして、政府と神社の関係の廃除、神道をその他の宗教と同等に扱うこと、神祇院の廃止、学校教育及び公共機関からの神棚の撤去、学校から神社への訪問の停止などが含まれた（HARDACRE, 51-52)。

　1945年12月15日「神道道令」がだされ、1946年1月末に神祇院は廃止され、神社は国家管理をはなれることとなる（Nishimura, 18)。さらに、神道指令には、神道への公的資金の導入、神道の教義、慣習、儀式、儀礼における軍国主義的なイデオロギーの公布、神道及び神道関係者の育成に関わる教育機関の運営、学校教育における神道の教義の普及を、即刻停止することを命じた（HARDACRE, 51)

According to the section “Postwar Increase in Denominational Groups” in Religious Yearbook, 1950 Edition (compiled by the Ministry of Education’s Religious Affairs Division, published by the Bunkyo Association Foundation, 1951), one can observe how the number of religious organizations changed significantly over the four years following the enforcement of the Religious Corporations Ordinance (Nishimura, 22).

Under the Religious Organizations Law:

Shinto – 13,

Buddhism – 28,

Christianity – 2,

Shrines – 1

⇨ Total – 44

Postwar:

430 religious organizations,

Number of religious corporations – 9,491

(This figure represented only 5% of the approximately 200,000 total shrines, temples, and churches, and of this 5%, only a few percent belonged to new religious movements.)

In 1947, eight North American missionary organizations formed a Joint Commission for Missionary Work in Japan, and in August they held a Domestic and International Cooperation Conference with the United Church of Christ in Japan (Nishimura, 25). Meanwhile, Catholic missionary orders, which at that time were gaining entry into mainstream American society and actively expanding their overseas missions, were sending more missionaries to Japan than the Protestants (Nishimura, 26). At the request of Archbishop Marella, in September 1946, three young Japanese Catholic seminarians were sent to study at a pontifical university in Rome. During the Occupation period, ordinary Japanese citizens were prohibited from traveling abroad without permission from GHQ, and these three seminarians were the first Japanese students to be granted permission to study overseas under such circumstances (Okazaki, 53). Furthermore, regarding entry into Japan, missionaries were given “special treatment” (Okazaki, 56).

文部省宗務課編『宗教年報　昭和二五年版』（財団法人文教協会、1951）所収の「戦後の教派団体増加状況」では宗教法人令施行後の4年間で、宗教団体数が土曜に変化したかが伺える(Nishimura, 22)。

宗教団体法制下：神道13、仏教28、キリスト教2、神社１⇨合計44

戦後：430、宗教法人数：9491 （この数には社寺教会の総数約20万に対し、5%にすぎず、この新設法人のなかでも「信仰宗教団体に所属するのは5%のなかのさらに数%であった」）

1947年に北米の８つの宣教団が日本宣教のための連合委員会を組織し、8月には日本基督教団と内外協力会議を実施した（Nishimura, 25)一方で、ちょうど米国本土社会の主流に参入し、海外ミッションも積極的に展開していたカトリック宣教修道会の宣教師の方がプロテスタントよりも多く来日していた（Nishimura,26)また、マレラの要望で、1946年9月に、3名に和解日本人カトリック神学生がローマの教皇庁立大学へ留学することとなった。占領下の日本において、一般の日本人が海外に出国することは禁じられており、GHQの許可が必要であった。しかし、学生対する事例で最初に海外渡航が許されたのはこの3名のカトリック神学生たちでった（Okazaki, 53) また日本日本入国に関しても宣教師は「特別扱い」されていた（Okazaki, 56)

(SCAPIN-93)「政治的，公民的及宗教的自由に対する制限の撤廃に関する覚書」人権指令(1945) / SCAPIN-93: REMOVAL OF RESTRICTIONS ON POLITICAL, CIVIL AND RELIGIOUS LIBERTIES

(英語/ English)

OFFICE OF THE SUPREME COMMANDER
FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

4 October 1945
(SCAPIN-93)

Memorandum for Imperial Japanese Government
Through the Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.

Subject Removal of Restrictions on Political, Civi, and Religious Liberties

1. In order to remove restrictions on political, civil and religious liberties and discrimination on grounds of race, nationality, creed or political opinion, the Imperial Japanese Government will:
a. Abrogate and immediately suspend the operation of all provisions of all laws, decrees, orders, ordinances and regulations which:
(1) Establish or maintain restrictions on freedom of thought, of religion, of assembly and of speech, including the unrestricted discussion of the Emperor, the Imperial Institution and the Imperial Japanese Government.
(2) Establish or maintain restrictions on the collection and dissemination of information.

Social Hostility toward Christianity

Repression of the Holiness Movement (1942)

In 1942, repression was carried out against Holiness-related Christian churches. The targets were the Kiyome Church, Japan Holy Church, and the Oriental Missionary Society Holiness Church. Their eschatology interpreted God’s promises to Israel in the Old and New Testaments as being fulfilled in a “kingdom on earth” ruled by Jesus Christ (Kaminaka, 412). Moreover, the Holiness churches consistently maintained the position that “shrines are religious institutions.”

The reason for the arrests was that the “Second Coming belief” was considered a violation of the Peace Preservation Law (Kaminaka, 426).

A second wave of arrests began in April 1943, during which 36 individuals were arrested. Nationwide, 71 people were prosecuted, and 14 were sentenced to prison. In addition, the order to dissolve the Holiness churches was carried out as an administrative action by the Ministry of Education. In April 1943, the Ministry revoked church establishment permits under Article 16 of the Religious Organizations Law, while the Ministry of Home Affairs prohibited these associations under Article 8 of the Public Security Police Law. As a result, as many as 270 churches were dissolved. Based on this action, the Ministry of Education also administratively guided the pastors of the three Holiness churches to voluntarily resign (Kaminaka, 427).

However, looking at the notifications from the Ministry of Education, it remains unclear what aspects of the Holiness churches were seen as disruptive to public order (Kaminaka, 427). Doctrinally, the Holiness churches had very little to do with any attempt to alter the national polity. Yet, the Peace Preservation Law, by labeling them as “denial of the national polity” in terms of thought and belief, expanded the scope of repression to an extreme degree and made such crackdowns a priority.

If one were to draw out an implication about the nature of the emperor system from this episode, it would be that while the emperor system did not forcibly demand apostasy from churches, it also did not tolerate religious groups being “different.” The mechanisms that enabled this were the Religious Organizations Law and the Peace Preservation Law (Kaminaka, 449).

  1. ホーリネス系への弾圧（1942)

1942年にホーリネス系のキリスト教会への弾圧がなされた。対象となったのは、きよめ教会、日本聖教会、東洋宣教会きよめ教会であった。彼らは、旧新約聖書のイスラエルに対する神の約束は、イエス・キリストが支配する「地上の国」として成就するという終末理解であった（Kaminaka, 412）また、ホーリネスは一貫して「神社は宗教である」という主張を続けた。

ホーリネスが検挙された理由は、「再臨信仰」が治安維持法に違反すると考えられたためであった（Kaminaka, 426）。

1943年4月から第二次検挙がなされ、36名が検挙された。全国で71名が起訴され、14名に実刑判決がくだった。さらにホーリネス教会に対する解散命令は、文部省による行政処分として実施された。1943年4月文部省は宗教団体法第16条によって教会設立認可の取り消し処分を行い、内務省は結社に対して治安警察法第8条に寄って結社禁止処分をおこなった。この結果、解散した教会の数は270にものぼる。この処分に基づいて文部省は、ホーリネス系3教会の牧師に自発的辞任を促すよう行政指導をした（Kaminaka, 427）

しかし、一方でホーリネスの何が秩序を乱すものであったのかは、文部省の通知を見る限りはわからない(Kaminaka, 427) 教義的にもホーリネスは、国体の変革とはほとんど関わりのない組織であったが、それなのに「国体の否定」と思想・信条に加入してまでも、治安維持法は取締の対象を「広範囲」に拡大し、弾圧することを至上命令とした。ホーリネスとの関わりから天皇制の特質を上げるとすれば、天皇制は教会に棄教を迫りはしないがしかしまた異質であることを許さない。それを政治機構として機能させたのが、宗教団体法であり治安維持法であった。（449）

Resistance of the Non-Church Christians

As the Second Sino-Japanese War expanded, after 1941, surveillance and repression based on the Peace Preservation Law intensified against the non-church Christians (mukyōkai) who were carrying out missionary work through the publication of evangelistic magazines. The main figures among the non-church Christians who had their articles censored or publications banned by the authorities were as follows (Suzuki, 322): Yanaihara Tadao, Fujimoto Masataka, Ishihara Hyiei, Yamamoto Taijirō, and Saitō Sōjirō.

In 1937, Yanaihara Tadao’s essay “The Idea of the Nation,” published in the September issue of Chūō Kōron, was completely deleted by censors. Yanaihara’s thought was attacked by the right-wing thinker Minoda Muneki from 1937, who denounced Yanaihara’s views to the Ministry of Education’s Bureau of Religious Instruction and to the Ministry of Home Affairs. As a result, Yanaihara came under intensified surveillance by the police and military authorities (Ōtani, 134). Moreover, in October 1937, Yanaihara gave a lecture advocating pacifism, and when this was later published, it too became a problem. As a result, books he had written in the past were banned, and he was forced to resign from the university (Ōtani, 133).

Furthermore, Masaike Hitoshi, Suzuki Hikomi, and Watanabe Yaichirō continued to make anti-war statements and were arrested in June 1944 for violating the Peace Preservation Law (Suzuki, 323). Masaike Hitoshi’s Christian Pacifism was also banned on the grounds of being anti-war, with 500 copies confiscated (Ōtani, 133). In addition, Asami Sensaku was taken into custody and detained at the Sapporo police station in July 1943 for making “anti-war remarks.”

During the war, the only Christian group to engage in organized resistance was the Tōdai-sha (present-day Jehovah’s Witnesses); other cases of Christian resistance were individual efforts (Ōtani, 133).

Korea

With Japan’s defeat in the Pacific War in August 1945, the Korean Peninsula was liberated from Japanese rule. In the postwar period, Syngman Rhee’s government displayed a dual and shifting stance in its Japan policy. Beginning with the establishment of the government in 1948, South Korea pursued a hardline policy toward Japan, demanding reparations and expressing strong opposition to Japan’s rearmament. Additionally, the new Korean government aimed to eliminate Japanese influence (36).

In January 1949, South Korea established a diplomatic mission in Tokyo, and in April, the Japan-Korea Trade Agreement—comprising trade, financial, and trade planning agreements—was concluded (37). At that time, Japan was under Allied occupation, so this arrangement was essentially an agreement between South Korea and the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers (SCAP).

United States

Since the outbreak of the Second Sino-Japanese War, the United States had maintained a position of neutrality. Furthermore, because neither Japan nor China formally declared war—deliberately avoiding doing so in order to prevent the U.S. government from invoking the Neutrality Act—America was able to continue trade relations with both countries. However, as Japan expanded its occupied territories on the Chinese mainland and proclaimed the creation of a “New Order in East Asia,” the U.S. government began to feel the need to revise its Far East policy (Hattori, 247).

From 1938 onward, the U.S. called for a moral embargo by appealing to private companies, but under the terms of the 1911 U.S.-Japan Treaty of Commerce and Navigation, the U.S. was prohibited from imposing economic sanctions on Japan, making it difficult to enforce effective measures (Hattori, 248). However, as Japan moved to extend its military reach into Southeast Asia, both Britain and the U.S. began to apply more serious economic pressure on Japan, which led to growing shortages of essential materials within Japan.

Subsequently, the U.S. government notified Japan of the abrogation of the Treaty of Commerce and Navigation. In response, the Japanese government sought to counter this by trying to reduce its economic dependence on the U.S. and strengthen economic ties with Southeast Asia (Hattori, 266).

Furthermore, after the formation of the Second Konoe Cabinet in 1940, the U.S. government, just four days later, imposed an export license system on oil and scrap iron and, on the 31st of the same month, announced an embargo on aviation gasoline and certain grades of scrap iron. When the Tripartite Pact between Japan, Germany, and Italy was signed in September, the U.S. imposed a full embargo on scrap iron, and between December and January, the list of embargoed materials—mainly metals—was expanded step by step.

  • アメリカ

　日中戦争以来、アメリカは中立の立場をとっていた。さらに、米国政府による中立法発動を回避する目的で日中双方が宣戦布告をしなかったため、米国は日中双方との貿易関係を保っていた。しかし日本が中国大陸における占領地を拡大させ、東亜新秩序建設を声明するに至ると米国政府は極東政策に変更を加える必要を求めるようになっていった。(Hattori, 247)　1938年以降、米国は同義的禁輸を民間企業に訴えたが、1911年に締結した日米通商航海条約により相手国に経済制裁を課すことはできないと決められており、有効な制裁措置をとることができなかった（Hattori, 248) しかし、日本が東南アジアへの武力進出を進めるとイギリスとアメリカは対日経済圧力を本格化させ、日本の物資需給は逼迫していった。

　その後、米国は、日米通商航海条約破棄を日本政府に通告した。日本政府は東南アジア地域との経済関係悪化など経済的な対米依存状態の是正を測ることで、これに対抗した(Hattori, 266)。さらに、米国政府は、1940年に第二次近衛内閣が成立すると、その4日後に石油と鉄屑に対しても輸出許可制をとり、同31日には航空ガソリンと一休鉄屑の禁輸を発表した。そして9月に日独伊三国同盟の締結が行われると、米国政府は屑鉄の全面禁輸措置を取り、以後12月から1月にかけて金属類を中心に対日禁輸物資を次々と拡大させていった。

The U.S. government sought through these measures to restrain Japan’s southward expansion policy (Hattori, 267). Japan attempted to secure military supplies, but negotiations stalled. In July 1941, Japan proceeded with the deployment of troops into southern French Indochina, to which the U.S. government responded with a complete oil embargo and the freezing of Japanese assets. As a result, Japan effectively entered into a state of economic severance from the United States (Hattori, 267).

The subsequent so-called “ABCD encirclement” was a narrative manufactured by Japan to inflame public anxiety; in reality, the U.S. sanctions were aimed solely at forcing Japan to abandon its southward expansion policy. However, this strategy had the opposite effect, hardening Japan’s stance and further intensifying its expansionist pressures (Hattori, 267). The fundamental cause of this U.S.-Japan conflict was a rivalry over interests in China, and the escalation of these frictions ultimately triggered the war.

Japan, which relied heavily on the U.S. for industrial resources, began to suffer economically. The Japanese government believed that the Nazi victories in Europe had weakened European powers in Asia, creating an opportunity for Japan to expand its sphere of influence at minimal cost by filling this vacuum (Minohara, 270–271).

In this situation, two Catholic missionaries from the Maryknoll Mission in New York State—Fathers James E. Walsh and James K. Draught—came to Japan with the aim of negotiating a U.S.-Japan peace agreement. They called themselves the “John Doe Associates” and acted secretly and anonymously. Behind their efforts was Postmaster General Frank C. Walker, a Catholic and personal friend of President Roosevelt (Minohara, 272). Because Catholics were traditionally anti-communist, they feared that a U.S.-Japan conflict would allow the Soviet Union to take advantage, and they strongly desired U.S.-Japan cooperation as a means of countering what they saw as the greater threat posed by the Soviets (Minohara, 272).

　米国政府はこうした措置によって日本の南進政策を抑制しようとしたのであった（Hattori, 267)日本は軍需物資の確保を図るも交渉は難航し、1941年7月南部仏印への兵力進駐が実されたが、これに対し米国政府は石油の全面禁輸と日本資産の凍結で応酬した。これによって日本は事実上の対米経済断行状態に陥った（Hattori, 267)。その後のABCD包囲網はあくまでも日本側が危機感を煽るために作りだしたものであり、米国はあくまでも日本の南進政策の放棄を迫る目的で経済制裁を発動した。しかし、この方策は、むしろ日本の態度を硬化させ、対外膨張圧力を強化させる結果を招いた（Hattori, 267)　このような日米の衝突の根本的な問題は、中国をめぐる利権対立であり、日米摩擦の拡大が戦争の引き金となった。

その後、工業資源の大部分を米国からの輸入に頼っていた日本は、徐々に経済的に苦しくなっていった。日本はヨーロッパでのナチスドイツの勝利によって敗北した国々がアジア域内でのパワーが減退し、その空間を日本が埋めれば少ないコストで勢力圏を拡大できると考えていた（Minohara, 270-271)

このようななかで、日米関係を憂慮したカトリックの海外伝道師協会に所属するニューヨーク州メリノール教会のウォルシュ（JamesE. Walsh)とドラウト(James K.Draught)神父の2人が日米和平協定の締結を目指して来日した。彼らは自らを(John Doe Associates」と呼び、極秘、匿名で両密使の後ろ建てとなっていたのは、大統領と個人的に親しく自らもカトリック教徒のFranck C. Walker郵政長官であった(Minohara,272)。カトリック教徒は伝統的に反共主義者であったため、日米衝突となればソ連が漁夫の利を得ることを危惧し、より大きな脅威として認識していたソ連を牽制するため、日米の連携を切望した（Minohara, 272)

In parallel with this kind of private diplomacy, the Japanese government also sent a new ambassador to the U.S. in January 1941 to begin negotiations with President Roosevelt and Secretary of State Hull (Minohara, 272). However, Nomura’s poor command of English would later prove detrimental to the negotiations. Subsequently, Walsh, Draught, and Japanese representatives worked together to draft a “U.S.-Japan Understanding Proposal.” In November 1940, the “Draught Memorandum” was created, followed by the “Walsh Memorandum” in January 1941. After revisions to a “Draft Agreement in Principle” in March, the “U.S.-Japan Understanding Proposal” was finalized in April. However, the Japanese government mistakenly assumed that this proposal had been accepted by the U.S. government.

Later, the U.S. government presented a much tougher proposal based on Hull’s “Four Principles,” which greatly shocked the Japanese side due to the gap between their expectations and reality (Minohara, 272–273). The State Department leadership had not been involved at all in the formulation of the 1940 “U.S.-Japan Understanding Proposal,” and strongly anti-Japanese figures were passionately determined to reject it, making its acceptance virtually impossible (Minohara, 273).

On October 18, 1941, the Tōjō Cabinet was formed, and U.S.-Japan negotiations entered their final phase. In the U.S., it was perceived that the peace faction had been completely purged from the Japanese government and that a cabinet willing to pursue war with the U.S. had finally come to power (Minohara, 274). However, because Foreign Minister Shigenori Tōgō—known for favoring cooperation with the U.S.—had been appointed, the U.S. government, relying also on reports from its ambassador in Japan, chose to closely monitor the Tōjō Cabinet’s actions for the time being (Minohara, 274). It was at this point that renewed diplomatic efforts began, aiming for a compromise to break the deadlock in U.S.-Japan negotiations.

After 1947, influenced by the emerging East-West Cold War structure in East Asia, the U.S. reversed its previous “democratization” policy. In August 1950, the National Police Reserve was organized, marking the beginning of Japan’s rearmament.

Germany

From 1938 onward, Germany’s dismantling of the Versailles system accelerated. Germany annexed Austria, then the Sudetenland, and later Moravia, with its next territorial ambitions directed toward Poland. The Japanese government observed these developments in Germany and sought to strengthen ties with Germany in order to negotiate more effectively with Britain and France, who were supporting the Chinese Nationalist government.

In this context, on September 27, 1940, the Tripartite Pact (Berlin Pact) was concluded between Japan, Germany, and Italy as a military alliance against Britain and the United States. Behind this alliance was Japan’s vision for establishing the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere. Within its plan for a “New Order in East Asia,” Japan intended to take over the Southeast Asian colonies of the Western powers defeated by Germany and Italy in the European theater, and to integrate these with Japan’s own New East Asian Order, thereby creating a more autonomous Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere. This was one of the key objectives behind entering into the alliance (Hattori Satoshi, “66 The Tripartite Pact,” 262).

  • ドイツ/ Germany

1938年以降、ドイツによるヴェルサイユ体制解体が本格化し、ドイツはオーストリアの併合、ズデーテン併合、そしてモラビアを併合し、次の領土的野心はポーランドへと向かっていた。日本政府はこのようなドイツの状況を観察し、ドイツとの関係を強化することで、国民政府を支援するイギリス、フランスとの交渉を画作した。

　このようななかで、1940年9月27日、日独伊三国同盟（Tripartite Pact/ Berlin Pact)が締結された。この同盟は、対英米軍事同盟として日本、ドイツ、イタリアによって成立したが、この背景には大東亜共栄圏の確立という構想があった。日本は、東亜新秩序の計画においてヨーロッパ戦線でドイツイタリアに降伏した西洋諸国の東南アジア植民地を接収し、日本の東亜新秩序と組み合わせることでより自立性の高い大東亜共栄圏を確立するという目的があり、そのための同盟でもあった。（服部聡、「66　日独伊三国同盟」262)

Soviet Union

Since the Nomonhan Incident of May 1939, hostilities between Japan and the Soviet Union had continued. Japan had been negotiating with the Soviet Union over rights in northern Sakhalin and a non-aggression treaty with Japan, but neither side was willing to compromise. Ultimately, it was agreed to defer the issue of northern Sakhalin to future negotiations, and in April 1941, Japan and the Soviet Union signed the Soviet–Japanese Neutrality Pact (Kenjiro Shibuya, 268).

This treaty, with a term of five years, pledged the maintenance of peaceful and friendly relations between the two countries, respect for territorial integrity and non-aggression, and the commitment of each side to maintain neutrality if the other was subjected to military action by a third party (Shibuya, 268).

However, in February 1945, Germany notified Japan of its intention to annul the treaty. After Germany’s unconditional surrender, as Japan’s situation deteriorated, Japan asked the Soviet Union to mediate peace negotiations with the United States. The Soviets, however, stalled for time and, on the very day of the atomic bombing of Hiroshima, declared war on Japan (Shibuya, 269)—even though the treaty was still in effect until April 1946, making this action a violation of the pact.

Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere

The concept of the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere emerged in Japan after 1940, influenced by the course of the war in Europe. The idea was to seize the Southeast Asian colonies of Britain in addition to the already-defeated Dutch and French colonies, incorporate them into the “New Order in East Asia,” and thereby create an economically self-sufficient bloc (Hattori, 276). This was the basic vision behind the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere, and Foreign Minister Yōsuke Matsuoka promoted the southward expansion policy in pursuit of this goal (Hattori, 276).

Matsuoka justified the southward expansion by presenting the construction of the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere as an effort to achieve the coexistence and mutual prosperity of Asian peoples (Hattori, 276). Behind this rhetoric lay the dissatisfaction and resentment of Southeast Asian peoples toward Western colonial rule, which Japan sought to exploit by appealing to ideas of colonial liberation and Asian solidarity, in order to gain local support (Hattori, 277).

Furthermore, after July 1941, as a result of the U.S., British, and Dutch governments imposing a full oil embargo and freezing Japanese assets, Japan sought resources in Southeast Asia. Under the pretext of self-defense, Japan declared war on the U.S., Britain, and the Netherlands and launched its invasion of Southeast Asia (Hattori, 277).

Thus, the true aim of constructing the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere was to establish an economically self-sufficient bloc, while the slogan of colonial liberation served merely to justify this goal. Although Japan was initially welcomed as a liberating force, Japanese occupation rule soon devolved into the plundering of resources and forced labor. Moreover, Japan’s rule came to resemble that of the Western colonial powers…

  • 大東亜共栄圏/Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere

大東亜共栄圏は、1940年以降のヨーロッパ戦争の戦況から日本国内において、すでに降伏したオランダとフランスの植民地に加え、イギリスの東南アジア植民地を奪取し、東亜新秩序に加えることで、自立性の高い経済圏を作り上げる構想が浮上した。(Hattori, 276)これが大東亜共栄圏の基本構想であり、この建設を目指して、南進政策を推進したのが松岡洋右外相であった（Hattori, 276)。

　松岡は、南進政策の目的はアジア民族による共存共栄を目指す大東亜共栄圏の建設だとして、その正当性をはかった(276) 。その背景には、西洋諸国の植民地支配に対する東南アジアの人々の不満と反発があり、こうした状況を捉えて植民地解放とアジア民族の共存共栄を唱えることで、東南アジアの人々の関心を取り付けようとした（Hattori, 277)

さらに、1941年7月以降、米国政府やイギリスオランダ領による石油全面禁輸と資産凍結の結果、日本は東南アジアに資源を求め、自陣自衛を名目にアメリカイギリスオランダに対し宣戦布告し、東南アジアへの侵攻作戦を開始した。(Hattori, 277)

そのため、大東亜共栄圏の建設目的は経済自給圏を作ることであり、植民地解放はこれを正当化するための「名目」に過ぎなかった。当初は解放軍として歓迎された日本であったが、その後日本による占領統治は物資収奪や強制労働に終始した。さらに日本による統治は西欧諸国の統治よりも劣悪であるとして抗日ゲリラが発生するようになった。（Hattori, 277)
