Japan: Context (1990-2000)

Governmental Regulations on Religion and Legal Cases

Revision of the Religious Corporations Act (1995) and Enactment of the Group Regulation Law (1999)

Following the 1995 Tokyo subway sarin gas attack carried out by Aum Shinrikyō, the Religious Corporations Act was revised in the same year (1995), and in 1999, the Act on the Control of Organizations That Have Committed Indiscriminate Mass Murder (commonly known as the Group Regulation Law) was enacted.

Up to that point, regulations concerning religious organizations had been relatively lenient, due to reflections on prewar state control and a strong commitment to freedom of religion. However, after the Aum incident, state regulation and intervention in religious groups became possible.

⸻

Revision of the Religious Corporations Act (1995)

The 1995 revision strengthened regulations on the operation of religious corporations. It included mandatory disclosure of information regarding their activities and financial accounting, thereby increasing transparency and accountability.

⸻

Group Regulation Law (1999)

This law was enacted to regulate organizations that have committed indiscriminate mass murder. It enabled government authorities—specifically the Public Security Intelligence Agency—to conduct investigations, issue dissolution orders, and take other regulatory actions against such organizations.

宗教法人法の改正（1995)と団体規制法（1999)の制定/ Revision of Religious Corporations Act and Enactment of the Group Regulation Law

1995年のオウム真理教による地下鉄サリン事件以降、同年に宗教法人法が改正され、1999年には団体規制法（無差別大量殺人好意を行った団体の規制）に関する法律が制定された。　

これまで戦前の反省や、信教の自由の観点から宗教団体に関する規制は緩やかなものであった。しかし、オウム真理教の事件以降、国家による宗教団体への規制や介入が可能のとなった。

宗教法人法の改正（1995)

宗教法人の活動内容や会計に関する情報公開を義務付けるなど、宗教法人の運営に対する規制が強化された。

団体規制法（1999)
無差別大量殺人行為を行った団体を規制するための法律が制定された。これにより、公安調査庁による調査や解散命令などの措置が可能となった。

Reactions from the Christian Community:

On October 17, 1995, the Catholic Central Council of Japan issued a statement on its website titled “The Catholic Church’s Position on the Revision of the Religious Corporations Act.” In the statement, the Council expressed that the government’s proposed revision was not something they could endorse wholeheartedly. It argued that the Aum Shinrikyō incident could be addressed under existing laws, including applying dissolution orders under the current Religious Corporations Act. The statement emphasized that religion is indispensable to the Japanese people, and that the proposed revision failed to accurately grasp the diverse realities, roles, and challenges of religious corporations. The Council therefore called for “careful deliberation over time” (Ojawara, 220).

キリスト教界の反応：

カトリック中央協議会は1995年10月17日に「「宗教法人法改正」に関するカトリック教会の考え」と題する声明をホームページ上で発表した。ここには政府の改正案について「両手をあげて賛同できるものではない」表明し、オウム真理教事件は現行法で対応しうるものであり、宗教法人法の解散命令などを適用すべきとし、宗教は日本人にとって欠かすことのできないものであり、改正案が宗教法人の多様な実態と役割、課題を正確に把握したものではないとした上で「時間をかけた慎重な審議」を求めた（Ojawara,220)

Reactions from New Religious Organizations:

The Federation of New Religious Organizations of Japan (Shinshūren) established a “Committee for Examining Issues Related to the Review of the Religious Corporations Act” in June 1995. The committee engaged in ongoing discussions on the matter, and in September, submitted an opinion paper to the Minister of Education. The document opposed a hasty revision of the Act, and urged that any reconsideration should be conducted calmly and with broad consultation.

新宗教団体の反応：　

　新日本宗教団体連合会(新宗連）は1995年6月に、「宗教法人法見直し問題検討委員会」を設置し、同問題に対して議論を重ね、9月には文部大臣宛に、宗教法人法の性急な改変に反対であること、また見直しは冷静な環境で衆知を集めて議論すべきとの「意見書」を提出した。

In March 1996, the Federation of New Religious Organizations of Japan (Shinshūren) announced its intention to promote the following action plan, while fundamentally upholding freedom of religion as a human right and the principle of separation between religion and state, in response to societal expectations.

Action Plan:

• Enhancement of Self-Governance of Religious Corporations:

Strengthen the ability of each denomination to govern itself appropriately in all respects, based on its own doctrines and traditions. A “Religious Corporation Administrative Consultation Office” will be established within the secretariat.

• Promotion of Social Contribution Activities:

Member organizations and Shinshūren as a whole will examine the public benefit and social role of religious corporations and promote social contribution activities across a wide range of areas.

• Research on the Religious Corporations System and Related Issues:

Centered around study groups such as the Religious Corporations Study Group, research will be conducted on:

1. The appropriate structure of the religious corporation system

2. The relationship between religion and politics

3. The current religious landscape in Japan and abroad

4. Promotion of Interreligious Dialogue:

To encourage dialogue and cooperation within the religious community and strengthen trust with society, Shinshūren will establish a “Religious Dialogue Office” within its headquarters.

　また新宗連は、1996年3月に基本的に人権に関わる信教の自由と政教分離の原則を守りつつ、社会の要請に答えるために下記の行動計画を策定し、推進していくことを表明した。

宗教法人の自治能力の向上：各教団の教義や伝統に基づき適切な法人運営とあらゆる面での自治能力を高める。事務局内に「宗教法人事務相談室」を設置する。

社会貢献活動の推進：各加盟団体及び新宗連として、宗教法人の公益性や社会的役割について検討し、幅広い領域で社会貢献活動を推進する。

宗教法人制度などの研究：宗教法人研究会などが中心となり（1)宗教法人制度のあり方、(2)宗教と政治の関わり方、(3)日本及び海外の主教事情について研究を行う。

宗教対話の推進：宗教界における対話と協調をすすめ、社会との信頼関係を高めるために、新宗連会館内に「宗教対話室」を設ける。

Article 78-2 (Reports and Inquiries)

1. When the competent authority determines that there is a suspicion that a religious corporation falls under any of the items listed below, it may, to the extent necessary for the enforcement of this Act, request the religious corporation to submit reports concerning the management and operation of its affairs or activities, or may have its staff question the representative officers, responsible officers, or other related persons of the religious corporation.

Geopolitical Dynamics and International Relationships

1995年は戦後50年の年であり、日本の様々な場面で、戦後50年を記念したり、振り返る企画が持たれた。キリスト教会ないでは、多くの今日は教団から、50年にあたっての平和声明や戦争責任告白声明が発表された。

Evangelicals:

In June 1995, the Japan Evangelical Association (JEA) adopted and issued a statement titled “JEA Statement on the 50th Anniversary of the End of the War” at its 10th General Assembly. Up to that point, Evangelical churches in Japan had largely refrained from speaking out about the war responsibility of Japanese churches. Among them, Holiness churches in particular tended to see themselves primarily as victims of wartime persecution, and thus felt less compelled to acknowledge responsibility (Nakamura, 239).

Moreover, many Evangelical denominations were newly established in the postwar period, making it more difficult for them to perceive war responsibility as personally relevant (Nakamura, 239).

Nonetheless, the JEA confessed that the sins committed by the United Church of Christ in Japan (UCCJ) were, in fact, sins that “we, the churches of Japan as a whole” committed. It acknowledged that the churches had failed to genuinely repent of those sins even afterward. In the context of issues such as the Yasukuni Shrine controversy and the imperial succession, the JEA declared a new awareness of Evangelicals’ own responsibility for the war, and expressed an apology for the complicity of Japanese churches in the war of aggression against Asian nations, seeking their forgiveness (Nakamura, 239).

This year marked a turning point, after which many Evangelical denominations issued statements regarding their war responsibility. Notably, the Japan Evangelical Christian Association (JECA) released a statement in April 1995 titled “Our Responsibility Concerning the Guilt of the Japanese Church in World War II,” reflecting the self-examination of a denomination founded in the postwar period.

Statements were also issued by denominations descended from Holiness churches, such as the Brotherhood of Christ in Japan (Kirisutokyō Dōshikai) and the Japan Holiness Church, both of which had been persecuted during the war (Nakamura, 240).

In March 1996, the Brotherhood of Christ in Japan adopted a clergy resolution titled “Repentance for Past Guilt and Determination for the Future.” In 1997, the Japan Holiness Church issued a statement at its General Assembly titled “Our Confession Regarding the Japan Holiness Church’s War Responsibility.”

These declarations went beyond simply acknowledging guilt: they offered thorough confessions of sin, expressed a desire for reconciliation with other churches and the people of Asia, and affirmed a commitment to fulfill their moral and spiritual responsibility (Nakamura, 241).

福音派:

1995年6月、日本福音同盟は第10回総会において「戦後50年にあたってのJEA声明」を採択、発表した。これまで福音派の教会な日本の教会の戦争責任について積極的な発言をしてこなかった。なかでもホーリネス系は自らが弾圧を受けた被害者であるという人氏も強かった（Nakamura, 239)さらに福音派の多くの教派は戦後誕生した新しい団体が多くそうした点からも戦争責任は自分たちの問題となりにくかった(Nakamura, 239)。

そのため日本福音同盟が、日本基督教団が犯した罪を「私たち日本の教会が全体として」犯したものとして告白し、その後もその罪を積極的に悔い改めなかったことを認めた。そして、靖国神社問題から天王星の代替わりに至るなかで自分たちの戦争責任を自覚し、アジア諸国に対して侵略戦争に加担した日本の教会の罪を謝罪し、赦しを求めた（Nakamura, 239)　この年を境に、多くの福音派の教派から戦争責任に関する声明が発表された。なかもで「日本福音キリスト教連合」による「第二次大戦における日本の教会の罪責に関する私たちの責任」（1995年4月発表）など戦後生まれの教団の声明が見られた。また基督兄弟団や日本ホーリネス教団など戦時下で弾圧されたホーリネスの後身にあたる団体からも聖目が出された（Nakamura, 240)。1996年3月、基督兄弟団は、「過去の罪責に対する悔い改めと将来への決意」を教役者決議として発表、日本ホーリネス教団は1997年の総会で「日本ホーリネス教団の戦争責任に関する私たちの告白」を発表した。これらは徹底した罪責の告白がなされ、戦争責任告白にとどまるものではなく他の教会の人々やアジアの人々と和解し、自分たちの責任を果たしていく決意が表明されていた（Nakamura, 241)
