Koyama Kōsuke ( 小山 晃佑)

Koyama Kōsuke ( 小山 晃佑 1929/12/10 – 2009/3/ 25)

  Birth place:Tokyo, Japan

  Dead place: Commonwealth of Massachusetts, U.S.A

  Education: Graduate from Princeton theological Seminary

Rev. Koyama was a Japanese Protestant Christian theologian.

Koyama Kosuke was born in Tokyo and graduated in 1952 from the Japan Christian Theological School, the predecessor of Tokyo Theological Seminary. During his studies, he was mentored by Kitamori Kazō, and afterward became a pastor (assistant minister) of the United Church of Christ in Japan.

In 1954, he graduated from Drew Theological Seminary in the United States, and in 1959 he earned his Doctor of Theology degree at Princeton Theological Seminary.

From 1960 to 1968, he taught at the Chiang Mai Theological Seminary in Thailand as a missionary of the United Church of Christ in Japan. In 1961, he was ordained as a pastor of the Church of Christ in Thailand.

From 1968 to 1974, he served as president of the Southeast Asia Graduate School of Theology in Singapore. In 1974, he became professor of theology at the University of Otago in New Zealand, and in 1981, professor of ecumenical theology at Union Theological Seminary in New York. His theological contributions were so significant that his death was reported in The New York Times with a photograph. However, within Japanese theological circles, he received relatively little recognition.

Among his major works are Water Buffalo Theology (Orbis Books, 1974) and Mount Fuji and Mount Sinai: A Pilgrimage in Theology (Orbis Books, 1984).

小山晃佑 は東京で生まれ、1952年に東京神学校の全身である日本基督教神学校専門学校を卒業した。在学中には北森喜蔵に師事し、その後日本基督教団の牧師（補教師）となった。

1954年に米国ドルウ神学大学を卒業し、1959年に米国のプリンストン神学校に留学し神学博士をを取得した。

1960年から1968年まで日本基督教団の宣教師としてタイのチェンマイ神学校にて教える。1961年タイ王国のキリスト教会牧師として、按手礼を受ける。

1968年から1974年シンガポール、東南アジア神学大学院学長を務め、1974年ニュージーランドオタゴ大学神学部教授、1981年ニューヨークのユニオン神学校のエキュメニカル神学の担当教授となった。彼の死はニューヨークタイムスでも写真入りで取り上げら得たことからも彼の神学的貢献の大きさがわかる。一方で、日本の神学会では評価されることは少なかった。

小山の著作として代表的なものがWater Buffalo Theology(『水牛神学』ORbis Books, 1974)やMount Fuji and Mount Sinai:A Pligrimage in Theology, Orbis Books, 1984『富士山とシナイ山ーひとつの神学的巡礼』などがあげられる。

M. Morse’s doctoral dissertation, Kosuke Koyama: A Model for Intercultural Theology (Merill Morse, Kosuke Koyama: A Model for Intercultural Theology, Peter Lang, 1991), identifies four major contributions of Koyama.

The first is his concept of the “Crucified Mind,” which draws on Lutheran theology. Koyama himself explored this theme in his own doctoral dissertation, expressing a foundational interpretation of how God acts within human history and what this means for human life (Kanda, p. 203).

The second contribution is to the theology of religions. In Thailand, Koyama examined other religions from the perspective of a theology of the cross, offering a fundamental framework for approaching this issue. His theology of the cross served as a key for Christians to encounter those of other faiths—the cross became a bridge, a tool, and a model through which Christians could relate to people of other religious traditions (Kanda, p. 203).

The third contribution is Koyama’s writing style, which brought a new dimension to contemporary theological discourse. The question of theological language or typology is a crucial matter for the formation of Christian theology across cultures. Koyama’s work demonstrates the existence of two types of theology: one is theoretical and abstract—characteristic of European-centered theology—while the other is shaped by story, symbol, and paradox—a more existential, narrative-based typology. Koyama’s theology clearly belongs to the latter, influenced by the style of Uchimura Kanzo, founder of Japan’s Non-Church Movement (Kanda, p. 203).

The fourth contribution is to Asian theology. Koyama contributed not only as an educator at theological schools in Asia but also as a passionate advocate and pioneer of contextual theology. As noted above, his theology of religions and his theological style were major contributions to Asian theology. One particularly important element was his “theology of the neighbor,” which has held significant meaning for Japanese churches and theology.

Koyama also made a major contribution to the global ecumenical movement as an ecumenical theologian. Especially from the 1980s onward, through lectures and articles for the World Council of Churches (WCC), he made an outstanding impact. His 1993 paper, “‘Welcoming the Other’: Mission Theology of the Cross” (International Review of Mission, vol. 82, 1993, pp. 283–295), which eloquently expressed the two hermeneutical cores of his theology—love of neighbor and the theology of the cross—was particularly well-received (Kanda, p. 204). Through such papers and keynote addresses, Koyama made a remarkable contribution to the global ecumenical movement (Kanda, p. 205).

M.モースの学位論文『小山晃佑ー文化間神学の一モデル（Merill Morse, Kosuke Koyama: A Model for Interecutual Theology, Peter Lang, 1991)には小山の４つの貢献について記されている。

１つ目は、ルターの神学に依拠する「十字架にかけられた精神(the Crucified Mind)」である。この課題は小山自身も学位論文で取扱い、神が人間の歴史において行動されるあり方と人間の生活に対するその意味についての基本的案解釈を具現化した（Kanda, 203頁）。

２つ目の貢献は、諸宗教の神学への貢献である。小山がタイにおいて、他の諸宗教を十字架の神学の観点から考察することを、この問題に対する根本的な見方ということができる。また小山の十字架の神学は、キリスト者が他の信仰に生きる人と出会う鍵となり、その十字架はキリスト者にとって信仰に生きる人々と接点を持つ一つの橋であり、道具であり、モデルであった（Kanda, 203).

３つ目の貢献は、神学の現代的な出会に対する小山の文章スタイルの貢献である。このような神学的言語、あるいは、進学的類型の問題は、キリスト教神学形成にとって文化間の次元の事柄である。この重要な事柄は、２つの進学の類型が存在することを示唆している。一つは神学の理論的、抽象的類型で、それはヨーロッパ中心の神学の類型であり、もう一つは、物語、シンボル、パラドックスによって特徴付けられている。より実存的な語りの類型である。小山神学は明らかに第二の類型に属しており、日本における無教会運動の創始者だる内村鑑三のスタイルから影響を受けている（Kanda, 203)

4つ目の貢献はアジア神学への貢献である。アジアの神学校の教育者として実践的に貢献してきているだけではなく、文脈化神学の熱心な擁護者であり、先駆者の一人として理論的にも貢献してきている。すでに述べてきたように、小山の諸宗教の神学と神学的スタイルはアジア神学への彼の主要な貢献である。なかでも重要な点は「隣人学」であり、日本の教会と神学にとって重要な意義を持った。

　小山は、エキュメニカル神学者として世界のエキュメニカル運動に大きな貢献を果たした。とくに1980年代以降のWCCにおける講演や論文の投稿において大きな貢献をし、なかでも1993年に発表された、「『他者をもてなす』─十字架の神学の宣教学」（IRM,82 1993, pp.283-295)は小山神学の隣人あいと十字架の神学という２つの解釈学的な核が見事に表現された内容で反響を呼んだ論文であった（Kanda, 204)そのため、小山は論文及び基調講演を通して、世界のエキュメニカル運動に際立った貢献を果たしてきた（Kanda, 205)

The Rev. Dr. Kosuke Koyama, a theologian internationally known for using arresting metaphors drawn from his experience as a missionary to convey an influential vision of Christianity as compatible with Asian traditions, died on last Wednesday, in Springfield, Mass. He was 79.

The immediate cause was pneumonia, said his son Mark, who added that his father had had esophageal cancer.

Dr. Koyama, who taught at the Union Theological Seminary in Manhattan, strove to make the teachings of Christ culturally meaningful to Asians, without sacrificing the essential Gospel message. His 1974 book, “Water Buffalo Theology,” was “one of the first books truly to do theology out of the setting of Asian villages,” Donald Shriver, president emeritus of Union, said in an interview on Tuesday.

In an online review, Herb Swanson, historian for the Church of Christ in Thailand, called the book “one of the classic works of contemporary Asian theology.”

Ichiro Okumura (奥村一郎, 1923/ -2014/6/4)

奥村はカトリック教会の聖職者であり、カルメル会の修道司祭である。

Birth Place: Gifu

Dead place:

Education: Graduate from Tokyo Imperial University

While still a student at the former higher school (kyūsei kōtō gakkō), he studied under Nakagawa Sōen of the Rinzai Zen tradition. In 1948, he graduated from the Faculty of Medicine and Law, Department of Political Science, at the University of Tokyo, and then entered the Department of Religious Studies in the Faculty of Letters.

A turning point for him came in December 1948 when he was baptized at Ōmori Catholic Church. In preparation for baptism, he studied the catechism under Father Masayoshi Shimoyama at Ōmori Church and then received baptism. While gathering materials for a university report on religious studies, he came across The Ascent of Mount Carmel, which introduced him to the Carmelite Order. Later, his participation in a gathering in Nagoya and his encounter with Saint Thérèse of Lisieux confirmed his path toward joining the Carmelites (p. 17).

In 1951, after graduating from the Department of Religious Studies at the University of Tokyo, he traveled to France to enter the Carmelite Order, a Catholic religious order. He was ordained a priest in Rome in 1957 and returned to Japan in 1959. Since then, he devoted himself to Buddhist-Christian dialogue and writing, and from 1979 served as a theological advisor to the Pontifical Council for Interreligious Dialogue.

旧制高等学校在学中から臨済宗の中川宗淵に師事した。1948年に東京大が医学法学部政治学科を卒業し、次に文学部宗教学科に入学した。

　彼にとっての転機は、1948年12月カトリック大森教会において洗礼を受けたことである。奥村は洗礼を受けるために、大森教会の下山正義神父のもとで、公共要理を学び、その後、洗礼を受けた。さらに大学での宗教学のレポートを各ため配布資料に借りた『カルメル山登学』がカルメル会との出会いとなり、その後、名古屋で開催された集会に参加しリジューの聖テレジアとの出会いが、カルメル会への道を決定づけた（17)

1951年東京大学文学部宗教学科を卒業し、カトリック修道会であるカルメル会に入会するためにフランスに渡り、1957年、ローマで司祭に叙階される。1959年に帰国。以来、仏教との対話、執筆活動に従事し、1979年からバチカン諸宗教対話評議会顧問神学者を務めた。

日本福音同盟（Japan Evangelical Association: JEA)

Founded in 1968

JEA (Japan Evangelical Association) was established in 1968 as an organization for fellowship and cooperation among evangelical churches in Japan.

In 1959, separate centennial celebrations of Protestant missions in Japan were held by both ecumenical and evangelical groups. The evangelicals launched the “Centennial Bible Faith Movement,” and the following year founded the Japan Protestant Conference (JPC). JPC became one of the three founding member organizations of the Japan Evangelical Association, along with the Japan Evangelical Fellowship (JEF) and the Japan Evangelical Missionary Association (JEMA).

Before the formation of JEA, it had been difficult for evangelical churches to have a collective voice in the public sphere, as this was mostly channeled through the ecumenical National Christian Council (NCC), which did not represent evangelical perspectives. For this reason, JEA was formed to represent evangelical churches.

In the early years of JEA, the three founding bodies—JPC, JEF, and JEMA—worked in cooperation while maintaining their individual identities. They fostered fellowship based on biblical faith, collaborated in evangelism, and jointly addressed various issues.

In 1974, JEF took the lead in organizing the first Japan Congress on Evangelism, which played a significant role in promoting unity and cooperation among evangelical groups. In June 1972, JEA held its first two-week-long National Evangelism Consultation, which was attended by 230 participants. This meeting explored new directions for mission work in Japan and deepened fellowship among participants. Following this, the basic vision for the Japan Congress on Evangelism was announced, and at the 5th JEA General Assembly in 1973, the basic plan was formally approved. In June 1974, the first Congress on Evangelism was held, attracting participants from both Japan and overseas. During the congress, there were Bible lectures and workshops led by Dr. John Stott. The five-day congress concluded with the issuance of the “Kyoto Declaration.”

During the 1970s, the United Church of Christ in Japan (Nihon Kirisuto Kyōdan) was still suffering from internal divisions between its social activist and church-centered factions. At the same time, Kyōdan, which had produced martyrs during wartime repression, maintained a strong sense of pride and victimhood. In contrast, evangelical churches sought unity in opposition to Kyōdan and, learning from its instability, prioritized evangelism and pursued it energetically (Yamaguchi, 102). As a result, the 1970s were a period of significant growth for evangelical churches led by JEA.

JEAは1968年に創設された、日本の福音主義教会の交流及び協力期間である。

1959年にプロテスタント宣教百周年記念行事がエキュメニカル派と福音派それぞれで開催された。福音派は日本宣教百年記念聖書信仰運動を展開し、翌年に日本プロテスタント聖書信仰同盟（Japan Protestant Conference, JPC)が発足した。このJPCは、日本福音連盟（Japan Evangelical Fellowship, JEF)、日本福音宣教師団ともに、日本福音同盟の三創立会員の一つである。日本福音同盟が結成される以前は、エキュメニカル派のNCCを通してでしtk、教会の声を伝えることが難しく、福音主義を掲げる諸教会や教会を代表する機関はなかった。そのため、福音主義を代表する組織としてJFAを結成した。

創立からしばらくは、日本プロテスタント聖書信仰同盟、日本福音連盟、日本福音宣教師団の三者が、三創立会員制としてそれぞれ独自性を持ちながら、協力し合い、聖書信仰に立って交わりを育て、福音宣教に協力し合い、諸問題に取り組んできた。

1974年には、JFEが中心となって、第一回日本伝道会議が開催され、その後、純福音主義諸団体の一致強力において大きな役割を果たした。1972年6月、JEA主催の第１回宣懇談会が２週間にわたって開催され、230人が参加した。この会では、日本宣教のあり方が模索され、参加者の交流が深められた。その後、日本伝道会議の基本構想が発表され、1963年の第５回JEA総会では、伝道会議の基本計画が承認された。1974年6月、第一回伝道会議が開催され、日本国内外からの参加者があった。期間中には、ジョン・ストット博士による聖書後会や文化会などが行われた。5日間にわたる伝道会議は、最後に「京都宣言」を出して終了した。

1970年代、日本基督教団は、社会派と教会派による対立が生じ、いまだに尾をひいていた。

一方で、戦時中、弾圧の中で「殉教」者をだした、誇りと、被害者意識を強く持っていた。また日本基督教団への対抗から結束を強める傾向にあり、教団の混乱を反面教師として、伝道を優先し、これに邁進した（Yamaguchi, 102) そのため、日本福音同盟を中心とする福音主義派が大きく拡大した時代でもあった。

Anti-Christian Events/Movements

Abolition of the Department of Theology at Aoyama Gakuin University (Aoyama Gakuin University Theology Department Issue)

This term refers to the series of issues that arose at Aoyama Gakuin University, culminating in the decision by the Board of Trustees in March 1977 to abolish the Department of Theology in the Faculty of Letters.

The core of the controversy lay in the administration’s—represented by President and Board Chairman Kinjiro Oki—assertion that the Department of Theology was not adhering to the university’s management ideology (namely, “Christianity” as the “founding spirit” of the school), and the administration’s unilateral move to abolish the department (Arai, pp. 22–23).

During the 1960 “Anpo” (U.S.-Japan Security Treaty) protests, the university president (who also served as head of the board) issued a “Three Proclamations” in the name of the president, banning participation in the national student federation (Zengakuren), political activities, and strikes. Some faculty and students in the Department of Theology ignored these proclamations and joined anti-Anpo demonstrations.

In 1962, when some theology faculty objected to making the “Introduction to Christianity” course mandatory for all students, the university president reacted with fury. In 1966, a group of students—including those from the Department of Theology—organized the Shibata Lecture, and despite the university president’s explicit prohibition, proceeded to hold the event.

Osaka Expo Christian Pavilion Issue (万博キリスト教館問題) At the 1970 Osaka Expo, the National Christian Council of Japan (NCCJ) and the Catholic Bishops’ Conference of Japan agreed to jointly present a Christian Pavilion. However, as the Expo approached, strong opposition movements emerged within the United Church of Christ in Japan (UCCJ) regarding participation (Nakamura, 319).

Those in favor of participating in the Expo viewed it as a venue for the exchange of human culture and technology, and thus argued it was a valuable opportunity for evangelism to a large audience. Moreover, they emphasized its significance as the first ecumenical collaboration between Protestants and Catholics in Japan (Nakamura, 319).

On the other hand, the opposition argued that the Osaka Expo served as a platform for Japan to flaunt its economic power, which had achieved rapid and even alarming growth at the expense of the people of Asia, and to project Japan’s national prestige as a major scientific and technological power. They also pointed out that 1970 marked the year of the revision of the U.S.–Japan Security Treaty, and that state authorities were attempting to position this year not as “the year of security,” but rather as “the year of the Expo,” in order to mobilize the masses and manipulate public consciousness through the event. For the church to participate in such an Expo, they claimed, would be nothing short of collaborating with the regime’s criminal acts. Based on this reasoning, pastors, seminarians, and laypeople in the anti-Expo faction voiced their objections strongly in the UCCJ’s General Assembly, Executive Committee, and various regional synods (Nakamura, 319).

Behind the NCCJ’s efforts, it was in fact the Osaka Christian Council that took the lead in pushing for the Christian Pavilion’s participation (Dohi, 189). The Christian Pavilion at the Expo was ultimately carried out as planned, and by the end of August, visitor numbers surpassed 2 million. After the event, Christian Pavilion Director Jirō Nishimura was awarded a decoration from the Pope (Nakamura, 319). This controversy surrounding the Osaka Expo marked the beginning of a period of division within the United Church of Christ in Japan.

1970年の大阪万博で日本キリスト教協議会とカトリック司教協議会が協力してキリスト教館を出展することになった。しかし万博が近づくと日本基督教団の内部では参加をめぐり、激しい反対運動が展開された（Nakamura, 319)　万博への出展を巡って万博推進派は、万博を人類の文化や技術の交流の場として考えた。そのため、多くの来場者に伝道するチャンスであるとした。加えてプロテスタントとカトリックの初のエキュメニカルな協力の場として大いに意義があると主張した（Nakamura, 319)。 一方、反対派は、大阪万博は、アジアの民衆の犠牲の上に脅威的な高度成長を果たした日本の経済力を国内外に示し、科学技術において世界の大国となった日本の国威を宣伝する場である。さらに１９７０年は、日米安保条約改正の年にあたる。国家権力側は、この年を安保の年ではなく、万博の年と位置付けて、国民を大量動員し、大衆の意識を操作するために万博を最大限に利用しようとしている。そのような万博にキリスト教会が出展することは、体制側の犯罪的行為に加担することに他ならない。こうした論理に基づき、反万博グループの教職・神学生・信徒は教団総会、常議員会、各教区総会で自分たちの主張を強力に主張した（Nakamura, 319) 日本基督教協議会の背後には、大阪のキリスト教連合会が母体となってこの出展を推進した（Dohi, 189)。万博のキリスト教館は予定通り実施され、8月末には、キリスト教館の来場者は200万人を突破。そして終了後、西村次郎キリスト教館長は、ローマ教皇から勲章を授与された（Nakamura, 319) この大阪万博への出展をめぐり日本基督教団は分裂の時代を迎えることとなる。
